## The memory contradiction

The campaign accepted a memory design in `docs/archive/architecture-working/memory-loop-engineering.md` and `decision-delta.md` rows P-MEM-OWNER through P-MEM-CORRECT: a SQLite lifecycle store, automatic capture of lessons, a configured assessment route (a model judges an ordinary lesson), and consolidation.
NATIVE-09 (#120) builds the store and the assessment, NATIVE-10 (#121) the retrieval and delivery, OPS-11 (#137) the evaluation on real work, all behind CORE-08 and CORE-09.

`memory-design-2026-09-03.md` was written eight days before that design and never entered the repo.
Its ranked evidence points the other way on the two load-bearing choices:

- Finding 1 (five benchmarks): a memory bank continuously rewritten by a model rises, then degrades below no memory; raw episodic retention matches or beats every consolidator tested.
- Finding 6: a structured store wins on long haystacks; files win where memory is small, human-owned, and secondary, and files abstain better.
- Finding 7: frontier actors gain about zero task success from experience banks.
- Finding 8: cheap trajectory notes are the highest-return step; finding 11: a structural code index is the only intervention with a measured resolve-rate gain on a frontier model.
- Its net verdict: verbatim raw traces, lexical retrieval, a small human-curated layer, an applicability gate, and every automatic model-rewrite layer sandboxed behind git.

The accepted design's P-MEM-PROMOTE ("no human approval for every note", model-assessed activation) is the mechanism finding 1 measures as harmful.
Its P-MEM-RETRIEVE already agrees with the doc on the safe half (deterministic constraints first, ordinary search, no embeddings before measured benefit).
None of the three tickets cites any of the doc's 35 sources; the tickets cite two vendor cookbooks.

What this does not settle: the doc's citations are 2026 preprints and were not verified this pass, and the accepted design may have reasons recorded in a session transcript that the archive does not carry.
What it does settle: NATIVE-09 must not be built until the two designs are put side by side and one is chosen, which is the rule in `feedback_assess_next_ticket_before_building`.

## Picks, ranked by value to the goal

1. Reconcile the two memory designs before NATIVE-09.
   Write a one-page comparison in `docs/architecture-working/` that puts P-MEM-OWNER and P-MEM-PROMOTE against findings 1, 6, and 7, and record the choice in `decision-delta.md`.
   Default recommendation: build the doc's levels 1 and 2 (capture, recall, `memsearch`, about 120 lines of POSIX shell, zero model calls) as one Track C design issue, reissue NATIVE-09 as "assessment by a human at the weekly report, not by a model", and let OPS-11 decide whether anything above level 2 is earned.
2. Raw trace capture plus recall plus `memsearch` (M2, M3, M4) as that Track C issue.
   The doc ships runnable drafts.
   Cost: three scripts, three hook entries, one HARNESS.md risk line, one budget line.
   Law touched: the two-hook rule; the new hooks are lifecycle events, not tool matchers.
3. The lint-rule rule (R3): one line in `seed/docs/WORKERS.md`, "at the second recurrence of a pattern the docs already ban, add a deterministic check to `bin/check`", plus one shipped example.
   Track A.
   Same rule as `feedback_corrections_become_hooks`, now in the seed.
4. Auto-file bugs from a run (R7): `bin/factory` turns a worker's nearby-bug line into `gh issue create` with `needs-triage`.
   Fifteen to thirty lines.
   Fold into the next `bin/factory` ticket; one behavior change per session.
5. Cards and search-before-debug (M5, M6): extend backlog item 6 (decision ledger) rather than file new; must stay out of the always-on budget.

Not picked, with the reason: S1 and S2 (a non-programmer audience Loam does not serve); S5, S8 (no deploy path); M12 (owner config); M14 (vetting gate first); loop-drill (already covered by round 0 until a ledger says otherwise).
